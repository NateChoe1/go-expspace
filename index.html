Crasmaru's ko games encoding

Consider the following go problem. Black to play, with the goal of saving the bottom side group:

Black is locally surrounded; there is no way for black to directly connect the large black blob out to the center of the board where he could hope to make eyes. Black's only option is to aim at capturing one of white's groups, marked with squares. If black goes for that directly by playing at A, white will simply save by capturing a black stone. Therefore, black must save the stone first, although if black tries playing directly at B, white can capture in a ladder that runs into a ko. Therefore, black's only winning move is to capture the ko with C. This makes miai of the two ladders, if white fixes the ladder on the left side of the board, then black will run out the ladder on the right side of the board, and vice versa. Once black's stone is safe, black can come back and capture white stones.

We can extend this problem to add more kos and a more complex ladder. An example of such a problem can be found in Crasmaru's page on Sensei's Library (also viewable here). Let F1 be the boolean formula encoded in the left ladder, and F2 be the formula encoded in the right ladder. Black wins if, at the start of his turn, F1 OR F2. Similarly, white wins if, at the start of her turn, (NOT F1) OR (NOT F2).

Initially, it's black to play and both formulas are false. Black must capture some ko to turn both formulas true. Then, white must capture some ko to turn both formulas false. Both players continue capturing kos until at some point it becomes impossible to flip both boolean formulas, at which point the other player will run out the proper ladder and win the game.

The specific case where F1 = F2 — called the ko game by Robson, but which will hereon be referred to as the symmetric ko game — was shown to be EXPTIME-complete by Robson under Japanese ko rules (Australia National University, TR-CS-82-02, "Exponential Time Decision Problems Relating to KO-like Transition rules"). In Robson's setup, black is aiming for a direct win while white is aiming for a draw by triple ko. The necessity of a triple ko obviously means that this encoding doesn't work with superko. The primary contribution of this proof is the idea to use two different formulas to create stricter conditions on both players.

Motivating two formulas

This is a concept formed early in the process of developing this proof. For various reasons it didn't turn out to be very fruitful, but it provides a good pedagogical foundation for why having two different formulas may be valuable, and it provides a gadget that will be useful in a different context later.

The fact that our boolean formulas must be monotonic is a very strong restriction the setup of the ko game. To circumvent this, we may consider a simulated "ko variable", which is actually made up of three individual kos. In this case, if either player has control of (A AND C) OR (B AND C) they win. This restriction leads to the following three move forced sequence:

(;FF[4]GM[1]CA[UTF-8]AP[besogo:0.0.2-alpha]SZ[19]ST[0]AB[dd][dj][dp][ec][ee][ei][ek][eo][eq][fd][fj]AW[ep][fc][fe][fi][fk][fo][fq][gd][gj][gp]LB[bd:A][bj:B][bp:C];W[ej]TR[bj][bp];B[fp]TR[bd][bq];W[ed])

These three kos effectively act as a single variable. Once a player begins this sequence every move is forced until every variable is flipped and the turn is passed. Notably, ko C is always held by the player who doesn't have control over the variable, so a monotonic boolean formula can use ko C to simulate a NOT gate. There are many issues with this particular approach, one of which being that it must be possible to catch deviations from either player. One potential deviation would be to ignore your opponent's threat and play at some other variable. In the following diagram, ABC forms one compond variable and DEF forms another compound variable.

(;FF[4]GM[1]CA[UTF-8]AP[besogo:0.0.2-alpha]SZ[19]ST[0]AB[dd][dj][dp][ec][ee][ei][ek][eo][eq][fd][fj][np][oc][oe][oi][ok][oo][oq][pd][pj][pp]AW[ep][fc][fe][fi][fk][fo][fq][gd][gj][gp][md][mj][mp][nc][ne][ni][nk][no][nq][od][oj]LB[bd:A][bj:B][bp:C][rd:D][rj:E][rp:F];B[nj];W[ej])

Black's move at ko F is initiating a variable flip. White should respond at ko F to continue the flipping sequence, but white instead plays at ko B creating a threat of her own. Suppose that black were to challenge at this point; who would win? Both players have a set of kos which gives them a reasonable right to challenge and win. If we give priority to black, then any deviations by black would go unpunished. Similarly, if we give priority to white, then any deviations by white would go unpunished. We can fix this by having two different formulas, where one gives priority to black and the other gives priority to white. When one player deviates, the other player gets to choose which formula to get evaluated against and win.

The actual gadgets

This gadget is the 6 ko variable. It is a partisan gadget, this variable is controlled by black, who gets to decide when it gets flipped while keeping the initiative. Black wins if he holds ADEF OR ABEF OR ABCF. White wins if she holds DBC OR DEC. If black is currently holding all of the kos on the left column, then the variable is false. If black is currently holding all of the kos on the right column, then the variable is true. There is exactly one 6 move forcing sequence which flips the value of variable, and even in Japanese rules black is not allowed to flip the same variable twice in a row. Finally, there is exactly one moment in this sequence where it's white to play and she has both kos C and D, which allows white to detect when black is trying to flip a variable and make a challenge if he's not allowed to do so.

G3

G3 is an EXPTIME-complete game. There are two players X and Y, who each have a set of variables Xi..n, Yi..n, as well as a boolean formula FX(X, Y), FY(X, Y). On each move, players flip exactly one of their variables, and must satisfy their formula at the end of their turn. G3 is EXPSPACE-complete with superko.

Encoding G3

Each player has 2n+2 6 ko variables: One copy of your own variables, one of your opponent's, and two clock variables, both initialized to true. From here, black's variables in the ko game will be referred to with the BLACK_ prefix, and white's will be referred to with the WHITE_ prefix. Black's G3 variables will be referred to as X, white's will be referred to as Y, and the clock variables will be referred to as CLOCK_{X|Y}1..2, where X and Y correspond to black and white respectively. A single move in G3 will be played as follows:

  1. Black updates BLACK_CLOCK1
  2. If the old clock value was 1 (i.e. black has transitioned the clock from true to false), black updates one of his own variables.
  3. Black updates BLACK_CLOCK2
  4. If necessary, black updates his copy of white's variables to respond to white's previous move

We use the following compound formulas to enforce this sequence:

# BLACK_SENTE indicates that black is currently making his move, WHITE_SENTE
# indicates that white is doing so
BLACK_SENTE = [black has C on the latch]
WHITE_SENTE = [black has AB on the latch]

# terms which allow black to win if white deviates
BLACK_FORCING = BLACK_TRANSITIONING | BLACK_LATCHING | WHITE_SLOW | WHITE_FAIL | WHITE_BAD_TRANSITIONING

# terms which save black if white doesn't continue with the gadgets
BLACK_TRANSITIONING = BLACK_TRANS_BLACK | BLACK_TRANS_WHITE
BLACK_TRANS_BLACK = [black has ADEF OR ABEF OR ABCF for some black 6 ko variable]
BLACK_TRANS_WHITE = [black has DBC OR DEC for some white 6 ko variable]
BLACK_LATCHING = [black has AC or BC on the latch]
WHITE_SLOW = WHITE_SENTE AND [black has (ABC | DEF) for every white variable]

# terms which save black if white makes a mistake during her turn

T(VAR) = [white is transitioning her variable VAR, i.e. black has kos CD on VAR]

# formulas are evaluated with either ABC for a variable which is false, or DEF
# for a variable which is true
WHITE_FAIL = BLACK_SENTE &
             CLOCK_X1 = CLOCK_Y1 &
             (BLACK_X ≠ WHITE_X |
              CLOCK_Y1 ≠ CLOCK_Y2 |
              [white has failed to satisfy her formula] |
              ((CLOCK_X1 = 0 & BLACK_Y = WHITE_Y) |
               (CLOCK_X1 = 1 & BLACK_Y ≠ WHITE_Y))

WHITE_BAD_TRANSITIONING =
  (T(CLOCK_Y1) & (WHITE_Y ≠ BLACK_Y)) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y1) & (CLOCK_Y2 ≠ CLOCK_X1)) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Yi) & CLOCK_Y1 ≠ CLOCK_X1) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Yi) & CLOCK_Y2 = CLOCK_X1) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Yi) & (for some j≠i, WHITE_Yj ≠ BLACK_Yj)) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y2) & CLOCK_Y1 ≠ CLOCK_X1) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y2) & CLOCK_X1 & WHITE_Y ≠ BLACK_Y) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y2) & NOT CLOCK_X1 & WHITE_Y = BLACK_Y) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y2) & CLOCK_X1 & WHITE_X ≠ BLACK_X) |
  (T(CLOCK_Y2) & NOT CLOCK_X1 & WHITE_X = BLACK_X) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Xi) & CLOCK_Y1 ≠ CLOCK_X1) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Xi) & CLOCK_Y2 ≠ CLOCK_X1) |
  (for some i, T(WHITE_Xi) & (for some j≠i, WHITE_Xj ≠ BLACK_Xj))

# terms which allow white to win if black deviates. this is essentially the
# inverse of the above, except that when things are false that's good for white.
#
# these terms relate to white's win conditions, but are written from black's
# perspective, which converts AND gates to OR gates and vice versa.
WHITE_FORCING = WHITE_TRANSITIONING & WHITE_LATCHING & BLACK_SLOW & BLACK_FAIL & BLACK_BAD_TRANSITIONING

# terms which save black if white doesn't continue with the gadgets
WHITE_TRANSITIONING = WHITE_TRANS_WHITE & WHITE_TRANS_BLACK
WHITE_TRANS_WHITE = [black has (A|D|E|F) AND (A|B|E|F) AND (A|B|C|F) for some white 6 ko variable]
WHITE_TRANS_BLACK = [black has (D|B|C) AND (D|E|C) for white black 6 ko variable]
WHITE_LATCHING = [black has (A|C) and (B|C) on the latch]
BLACK_SLOW = [black has A|B on the latch] OR [black has (A|B|C) AND (D|E|F) for some black variable]

# terms which save white if black makes a mistake during her turn

T'(VAR) = [black is not transitioning his variable VAR, i.e. black has C|D on VAR]

BLACK_FAIL = BLACK_SENTE |
             CLOCK_X1 = CLOCK_Y1 |
              (BLACK_Y = WHITE_Y &
               CLOCK_X1 = CLOCK_X2 &
               [black has successfully satisfied his formula] &
               ((CLOCK_Y1 = 0 | BLACK_X ≠ WHITE_X) &
                (CLOCK_Y1 = 1 | BLACK_X = WHITE_X))

BLACK_BAD_TRANSITIONING =
  (T'(CLOCK_X1) | (WHITE_X = BLACK_X)) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X1) | (CLOCK_X2 = CLOCK_Y1)) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Xi) | CLOCK_X1 ≠ CLOCK_Y1) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Xi) | CLOCK_X2 = CLOCK_Y1) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Xi) | (for all j≠i, BLACK_Xj = WHITE_Xj)) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X2) | CLOCK_X1 ≠ CLOCK_Y1) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X2) | NOT CLOCK_Y1 | WHITE_Y = BLACK_Y) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X2) | CLOCK_Y1 | WHITE_Y ≠ BLACK_Y) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X2) | NOT CLOCK_Y1 | WHITE_X ≠ BLACK_X) &
  (T'(CLOCK_X2) | CLOCK_Y1 | WHITE_X = BLACK_X) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Yi) | CLOCK_X1 ≠ CLOCK_Y1) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Yi) | CLOCK_X2 ≠ CLOCK_Y1) &
  (for all i, T'(BLACK_Yi) | (for all j≠i, BLACK_Yj = WHITE_Yj))

F1 = BLACK_FORCING
F2 = WHITE_FORCING

The bulk of this set of formulas are the two compounts BLACK_FORCING and WHITE_FORCING, which are complementary of each other. Generally, BLACK_FORCING indicates that black is threatening a win, and WHITE_FORCING indicates that white is threatening a win. Because of our choices for our formulas, on each turn a player must both answer their opponent's threat and create a threat of their own. Note that the clock variables step through the following four step sequence with each G3 move:

Playing player CLOCKB CLOCKW I should flip a variable My opponent just flipped a variable
Black 0 0 No Yes
White 1 0 No No
Black 1 1 Yes No
White 0 1 Yes Yes

The following paragraphs will describe the game from black's perspective. Note that since BLACK_FORCING and WHITE_FORCING are complementary, this logic is entirely symmetrical for white.

First, note that neither player is allowed to cheat outside of the gadgets. If black initiates a 6 ko variable and white doesn't respond within the gadget, then black can challenge based on BLACK_TRANS_BLACK. Similarly, when white continues the 6 ko transition, black can't ignore white's responses. White can't deviate within a 6 ko transition either; any white move which is out of sequence within a 6 ko variable cannot influence the formula, except to set T(v) for that variable. If this result would lead to a white threat, then white would win anyways by simply playing out the normal transition sequence. If this result doesn't lead to a white threat, then black wins as white player is no longer making a direct threat. Any deviation from black within a 6 ko latch sequence also forfeits any direct threats, leading to a white victory. Also, on black's turn, black must either initiate a 6 ko variable or initiate the latch, as if black plays anywhere else (i.e. black tries to play on white's variables during his turn), white can punish based on BLACK_SLOW.

This means that the player who isn't currently making a G3 move is punished very quickly for any deviations. Every move by the current player yields an immediately urgent response, so the other player's only hopes are to either catch the current player on a bad transition or to wait for the current player to start the latch and pass the turn.

Catching cheating by the current player is harder. We'll take white to be the current player making a move on G3 and demonstrate how black can punish any deviation from the proper sequence. As demonstrated earlier, gadget transitions are atomic; any deviations within a gadget activation sequence are immediately punished by the loss of a threat against the opponent, so we may take a higher level look at the structure of the gadget activations.

On the first move, white must flip CLOCK_Y1. If white tries to flip any other variable, black can catch her with WHITE_BAD_TRANSITIONING. If black tries immediately flipping the latch, the final move will be illegal by superko.

On the next move, white may choose to flip one variable from WHITE_Y. White may not flip a second variable, as that would violate WHITE_BAD_TRANSITIONING. After white does so, white may not flip CLOCK_Y1 as now WHITE_Y ≠ BLACK_Y. White may not flip WHITE_X as she hasn't yet flipped CLOCK_Y2. White may not immediately flip the latch as after the latch has finished black can challenge on WHITE_FAIL.

After that, white's only move is to flip CLOCK_Y2. If white has flipped a variable in Y when she wasn't supposed to, black can challenge her based on the CLOCK values.

Finally, white's should update WHITE_X before passing the turn by flipping the latch. If white tries to update CLOCK_Y1 or WHITE_Y, black can challenge her based on CLOCK_Y2. If, after updating WHITE_X, white comes back to update CLOCK_Y2, black can challenge her due to the fact that white's previous move must have either introduced or removed a difference between WHITE_X and BLACK_X, which would make the observed values different from the expected values as from the CLOCK values.

Computational gadget verification
